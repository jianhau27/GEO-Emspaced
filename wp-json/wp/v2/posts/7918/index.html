{"id":7918,"date":"2026-09-23T18:09:27","date_gmt":"2026-09-23T10:09:27","guid":{"rendered":"https:\/\/emspaced.com\/?p=7918"},"modified":"2026-09-23T18:09:27","modified_gmt":"2026-09-23T10:09:27","slug":"lhdn-raised-rm4-4-billion-in-additional-tax-assessments-and-e-invoicing-is-strengthening-compliance","status":"publish","type":"post","link":"https:\/\/emspaced.com\/blog\/lhdn-raised-rm4-4-billion-in-additional-tax-assessments-and-e-invoicing-is-strengthening-compliance\/","title":{"rendered":"LHDN Raised RM4.4 Billion in Additional Tax Assessments \u2014 And e-Invoicing Is Strengthening Compliance"},"content":{"rendered":"

A figure announced at the 26th National Tax Conference in Kuala Lumpur on 4 August 2026 should be on every Malaysian business owner’s radar: <\/span>LHDN raised RM4.4 billion in additional tax assessments as of 30 June 2026<\/span><\/b> following its audit and compliance efforts.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

These are additional tax assessments arising from LHDN’s audit and compliance activities. LHDN has also expanded its use of technology and artificial intelligence in tax administration, including analysing more than <\/span>1.6 billion e-Invoices<\/span><\/b> to strengthen compliance and risk detection.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

Source: LHDN CEO Datuk Dr Abu Tariq Jamaluddin, speaking at the 26th National Tax Conference, as reported by The Star.<\/span><\/i>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

What e-Invoicing Has Changed About Compliance<\/span><\/b>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

e-Invoicing is giving LHDN access to more structured digital transaction information, allowing the agency to strengthen its compliance and risk-assessment processes.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

In April 2026, LHDN conducted a nationwide e-Invoice compliance review operation from <\/span>20 to 24 April<\/span><\/b>. During the operation, <\/span>108 taxpayers<\/span><\/b> were identified as having failed to implement e-Invoicing under Phase 1 and Phase 2 despite being required to do so.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

LHDN also reported that its review of e-Invoice data and subsequent compliance efforts resulted in <\/span>38,906 taxpayers<\/span><\/b> submitting income tax returns declaring <\/span>RM3.5 billion in previously unreported income<\/span><\/b>, involving <\/span>RM760.7 million in tax payable<\/span><\/b>.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

By June 2026, the same e-Invoice compliance programme had expanded to <\/span>52,540 taxpayers<\/span><\/b>, who had reported <\/span>RM4.07 billion in previously unreported income<\/span><\/b>. This represents the subsequent overall figure from the programme and <\/span>includes the earlier 38,906 taxpayers and RM3.5 billion reported in April<\/span><\/b>.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

This illustrates how e-Invoicing is becoming an increasingly important part of Malaysia’s digital tax administration and compliance framework.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

What Phase 4 Means for Your Business Right Now<\/span><\/b>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

For businesses with annual revenue or sales <\/span>above RM1 million and up to RM5 million<\/span><\/b>, the Phase 4 e-Invoice implementation date is <\/span>1 January 2026<\/span><\/b>.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

Businesses with annual revenue or sales <\/span>up to RM1 million are exempt<\/span><\/b> from e-Invoice implementation.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

HASiL has provided an <\/span>interim relaxation period until 31 December 2027<\/span><\/b> for taxpayers with a 1 January 2026 or 1 July 2026 implementation date. During this period, taxpayers are allowed to issue consolidated e-Invoices for transactions, subject to the requirements set out by HASiL, and no penalties will be imposed during the transition period where the stipulated requirements are met.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

The implementation date itself remains important: businesses should use the relaxation period to complete their e-Invoice implementation and ensure their systems and records are ready.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

For certain transactions, HASiL also requires an individual e-Invoice rather than a consolidated e-Invoice. For example, HASiL’s guidance states that from <\/span>1 January 2026, a single transaction exceeding RM10,000<\/span><\/b> requires an individual e-Invoice.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

What Audit-Ready Accounting Looks Like in 2026<\/span><\/b>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

Businesses should maintain accurate financial records throughout the year and ensure their accounting records are properly supported by their invoices and transaction documentation.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

With e-Invoicing becoming an increasingly important part of Malaysia’s tax administration, businesses should ensure their bookkeeping, invoicing, SST records, and supporting documents are properly maintained and ready for compliance reviews.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n
