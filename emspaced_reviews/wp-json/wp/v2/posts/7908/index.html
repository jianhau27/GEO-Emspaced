The mandate is live. The relaxation window is open. This is what compliance looks like in practice.<\/span><\/b>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

On 1 January 2026, Malaysia’s Phase 4 e-invoicing mandate came into effect. If your business has annual revenue between RM1 million and RM5 million, you are now legally required to issue invoices through LHDN’s MyInvois system \u2014 not as a PDF, not as a printed document, but as a validated digital file submitted directly to LHDN before it reaches your buyer.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

There is good news on the timeline. On 20 April 2026, LHDN published the updated e-Invoice Specific Guideline Version 4.7 and confirmed a further 12-month extension to the Phase 4 relaxation period. The penalty-free window now runs through <\/span>31 December 2027<\/span><\/b>, with full enforcement beginning <\/span>1 January 2028<\/span><\/b>.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

But \u2014 and this is the part many businesses are misreading \u2014 the relaxation period is not permission to do nothing. The legal obligation to issue e-invoices started on 1 January 2026. The relaxation period only means penalties under Section 120 of the Income Tax Act 1967 will not be imposed during this window, provided you have registered on MyInvois and are making a genuine effort to comply.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

What Your Accounting System Needs to Support<\/span><\/b>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

An e-Invoice in Malaysia is a digitally structured invoice submitted to LHDN’s MyInvois system for validation before it reaches your buyer. Unlike a traditional PDF or paper invoice, it must be in machine-readable XML or JSON format, carry a unique validation identifier assigned by LHDN, and sit within a government-monitored audit trail.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

This means your current accounting software needs to either support MyInvois API integration natively or connect through a certified middleware solution. Many Malaysian SMEs are still running older accounting software that does not support this \u2014 and the relaxation period is the right window to make that change without deadline pressure.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

One important rule to note regardless of relaxation period: any <\/span>single transaction of RM10,000 or above<\/span><\/b> requires its own individual e-invoice immediately, with no option to consolidate. This applies now, not from 2028.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

The Three Practical Steps Right Now<\/span><\/b>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

First<\/span><\/b>, confirm your compliance obligation. Businesses with annual revenue below RM1 million are generally exempt under the updated threshold. If your revenue falls between RM1 million and RM5 million, Phase 4 applies to you from 1 January 2026.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

Second<\/span><\/b>, register your business on the MyInvois platform if you have not already done so. Ensure your taxpayer identification number is active and your business details are accurate. This is a prerequisite for issuing e-invoices and a minimum requirement even during the relaxation period.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

Third<\/span><\/b>, assess your current accounting software. If it does not support MyInvois integration, 2026 and 2027 are the years to fix that \u2014 not the months before January 2028 enforcement begins. Businesses that implement properly during the relaxation period benefit from a longer testing window, cleaner MyInvois records, and significantly lower risk going into full enforcement.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

The Businesses That Will Struggle in 2028<\/span><\/b>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n

The relaxation period ends 31 December 2027. After that date, penalties apply and enforcement tightens with no further flexibility. The businesses that use this window well will enter 2028 with a working, tested system and trained staff. Those that treat the extension as a reason to delay will face the transition under pressure \u2014 which is exactly the scenario the relaxation period was designed to help them avoid.<\/span>\u00a0<\/span><\/p>\n
